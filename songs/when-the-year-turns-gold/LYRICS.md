# When the Year Turns Gold - lyrics (roles/lyricist.md)

Theme (never named in the song): she keeps a date he set when he left in the summer - "when the year turns gold" - and
waits at the ferry gate in autumn; he does not come. Original words; nothing of any standard is quoted or paraphrased.

Hook line: **"When the year turns gold"** - opens every A on the rising F4-Gb4-Ab4-Bb4-C5 (the long open "gold" on
the peak C5), unchanged three times, turned once in the last A ("and the gold turns gray"), twice in the tag.

Notes

- Circled, not named: the season and the waiting are shown by things (the train, the sandals left in the hall, the
  streets putting on their coats, the ferry gate at five, the coat he hated, the bench, the lamps). The only
  direct line is the bridge's turn, "I should have asked you to stay" - it earns its place on the melody's climb (in
  B2 "stay" is lifted to Db5, the song's top).
- Quotable lines: "Summer took the early train", "Leaves come down like letters, none of them from you", "and the
  gold turns gray".
- Few syllables: 5-7 per 2-bar line, ~1.0 syllables/s; every line ends on a long open vowel or a voiced coda (gold,
  said, five, red, shore, before, you, stay, new, on, gone, gray, away) and is followed by a rest (a breath).
- Stress: the stressed syllables sit on beats 1 and 3 or on the long notes ("YEAR turns GOLD", "FER-ry GATE at
  FIVE"); "and" in pickups is sung with its full vowel (`and{ae n}`) so the /d/ does not clog "and the".
- `lyrics.check`: the builds' diction lines pass (no `coda_short` / `coda_buried`; shortest coda "turns" /n/ 46 ms).
- Markup (singer expression): two leaned-on words per A - `*said*`, `*five*` (A1), `*hate*` (A2), `*you*`, `*stay*`
  (bridge), `*lamps*`, `*gone*` (A3), `*gray*`, `*still*` (A4) - and one intimate, near-whispered line, the last A's
  `(( it's not far away ))`.
- A&R round 1: "in the coat you never liked, like ..." (liked / like back to back) -> "in the coat you used to hate";
  "but the year was new" (odd beside a summer departure) -> "but I thought you knew".
